# Approved Style System

## Core identity

The approved look is a clean historical scientific-book illustration, combining copperplate engraving or early lithographic drawing with optional hand-applied watercolor. It should feel authored, observational, and educational—never like a photograph processed with a vintage filter.

## Line system

- Outer contours: thin, smooth, continuous black ink with controlled organic variation.
- Internal structure: economical and intentional; every line explains anatomy, material, direction, fold, or depth.
- Midtones: fine directional parallel hatching.
- Deep shadows: limited cross-hatching or sparse stippling.
- Light areas: untouched paper.
- Prohibited: fuzzy graphite, charcoal, uniform stipple, random scratch texture, dense marks on every surface, thick cartoon outlines.

## Color system

Color is applied beneath the complete black drawing.

- Use two or three transparent watercolor values per local color.
- Preserve black contours and hatching after color is added.
- Prefer restrained but readable local colors: ochre, russet, olive, sage, dusty rose, slate blue, warm gray, and natural skin tones.
- Use a neutral warm-white ground.
- Prohibited: global yellow or sepia wash, low-opacity haze, neon, glossy digital rendering, photographic color grading, airbrushed skin.

## Human figures

Use historical illustrated-book realism. A person may be recognizable, aged, expressive, or anatomically accurate, but must be constructed through drawing:

- design facial planes with selected contour and hatch lines;
- use a few intentional marks for eyelids, nose, lips, ears, and wrinkles;
- group hair into engraved locks or stroke masses;
- simplify fingers and clothing folds without losing plausibility;
- create volume through line direction and density.

Reject any result whose face depends on pores, soft lens-like gradients, specular skin highlights, photo-traced wrinkles, or modern portrait lighting.

## Black-and-white mode

- Pure black ink plus clean warm-white paper.
- No color wash and no gray texture overlay.
- All gray values emerge from spacing and direction of marks.
- Keep broad areas open so the image prints cleanly on worksheets.

## Annotation system

- Handwriting is neat, slightly imperfect, and clearly human.
- Use dark brown-black ink and thin leader lines.
- Keep notes outside important subject areas.
- Titles may be modestly larger; scientific names may be smaller and italic-like.
- Exact text accuracy is more important than decoration.

## Composition

- One dominant teaching subject or one clear ordered sequence.
- Generous negative space and restrained environment.
- Use small detail studies only when they explain a diagnostic or mechanical feature.
- For sequences, retain camera angle and geometry across stages unless a different view is pedagogically necessary.
