# Content Illustration

Create images that read as authored illustrations rather than photographs passed through a filter. Preserve the user's subject, teaching purpose, language, and requested color mode.

## Required workflow

1. Identify the teaching target and choose one mode: hand-colored engraving, black-and-white engraving, annotated natural-history plate, or construction sequence.
2. If color versus monochrome would materially change the deliverable and the user has not established a preference, ask. Do not invent a visual interpretation after the user has asked for clarification instead of inference.
3. Read [references/style-system.md](references/style-system.md). Read [references/prompt-patterns.md](references/prompt-patterns.md) only when composing an image-generation prompt. Use the smallest relevant reference image set described there.
4. For niche species, historical facts, anatomy, or engineering details, verify uncertain claims with an authoritative source before drawing. Keep in-image claims short.
5. Generate with an image-generation tool. When editing a supplied image, preserve the requested invariants explicitly.
6. Inspect the result against [references/quality-checklist.md](references/quality-checklist.md). Pay special attention to photo-derived faces, global texture filters, malformed anatomy, inconsistent construction geometry, and incorrect handwritten text.
7. If one defect is clear, retry once with that single targeted correction. If the defect persists, show the user and request direction rather than silently changing the style.

## Non-negotiable visual rules

- Fine black contour lines are the structural foundation.
- Form is explained by deliberate parallel hatching, limited cross-hatching, and sparse stippling—not photographic gradients or texture overlays.
- In color mode, retain visible black linework and place restrained transparent watercolor underneath it.
- In monochrome mode, use black ink on clean warm-white paper; apparent gray comes from line density only.
- Keep paper clean and neutral. Never simulate age with a yellow, sepia, tea-stain, grain, canvas, or distressed-paper layer.
- Use smooth, controlled, consistent strokes with small organic variation. Avoid fuzzy graphite and noisy micro-hatching.
- Keep compositions quiet, legible, and spacious enough for classroom use.
- Do not generate decorative filler, fake labels, signatures, or watermarks.

## Subject behavior

- **People:** Render historical-book realism: believable anatomy and identity, but deliberate illustrated shapes, economical facial lines, grouped hair strokes, simplified hands, and selected clothing folds. Never reconstruct a face through photographic skin shading.
- **Animals and plants:** Prioritize recognizable anatomy and diagnostic features. Use a large primary study plus smaller detail studies only when they improve recognition.
- **Objects:** Use observational contours and only the hatching needed to communicate material and form.
- **Construction:** Show one consistent structure changing through ordered stages. Preserve geometry between panels and label load paths, temporary supports, joints, or key components when relevant.
- **Handwritten notes:** Reproduce only exact supplied or verified text. Prefer 3–7 short lines with thin leader lines. Preserve spelling and Vietnamese diacritics; do not accept pseudo-text.

## Reference images

Bundled assets are style anchors, not subjects to copy. Do not copy their people, animals, diagrams, labels, poses, or compositions. Use them only for line discipline, watercolor behavior, annotation style, and page organization.
